# ADR-0012: OPEN — Venus Phase at Conjunction

- **Status:** proposed — OPEN, pending Ajq'ij review
- **Date:** 2026-06-10
- **Authority:** Flagged for Ajq'ij review — this is a lineage question, not an
  engineering decision
- **Supersedes:** —

## Context

The Venus phase dimension (ADR-0008) assigns one of two identities to a seeker based
on Venus's position at their birth:

- **Morning Star (Icoquih)** — Venus visible before sunrise: Pioneer
- **Evening Star (Xux Ek')** — Venus visible after sunset: Warrior

These two phases are separated by conjunction periods when Venus is invisible —
passing between Earth and the Sun (inferior conjunction, ~8 days) or behind the Sun
(superior conjunction, ~50 days). During conjunction, Venus is neither Morning Star
nor Evening Star; it is between identities, in transition, invisible.

Some seekers were born during these conjunction windows. The current architecture
assigns Morning Star or Evening Star based on Venus's computed position at birth,
but this assignment is astronomically correct only for non-conjunction dates. For
conjunction births, the code must either assign a phase (which may be wrong, or
meaningless, or theologically incorrect for the tradition) or acknowledge that the
question is open.

As with ADR-0011, this is not a technical edge case. It is a question about what
the tradition says about a person born during Venus's invisible passage — a
theologically and cosmologically significant moment in Mesoamerican thought.

## Why this is flagged, not decided

The same principle as ADR-0011: the code cannot avoid answering this. The v3 audit
surfaced it explicitly. The correct move is to ask the tradition before the code
answers by default.

## Current behavior (until this ADR is accepted)

Per ADR-0009 (fail-toward-silence), for any birth date where Venus is within the
inferior or superior conjunction window, the engine must not silently assign a phase.
The in-register response is: *"Venus was between its two faces at your birth — neither
the Morning Star nor the Evening Star, but in passage. What this means for your path
is something I will not name without knowing what the old words say about those born
in the invisible time. We can work with your other positions while this one remains
open."*

This posture treats conjunction birth as cosmologically significant rather than as
a gap to be papered over.

## Questions for Ajq'ij review

1. In the K'iche' tradition, is there a category or identity for someone born during
   Venus's conjunction — neither Icoquih nor Xux Ek'?
2. Is conjunction birth understood as a liminal or particularly significant condition?
3. Does the tradition have a protocol for determining Venus phase when the planet is
   not visible?
4. Are the Pioneer/Warrior interpretive frames the correct correspondences for
   Icoquih and Xux Ek' in K'iche' ceremonial understanding, or do they require
   revision?

## Consequences once accepted

- A superseding ADR will record the Ajq'ij's answer, their name and authority, and
  the date of the consultation.
- The Venus phase computation will be updated to handle conjunction births per the
  lineage guidance.
- The interpretive frames (Pioneer, Warrior, and any third category) will be confirmed
  or revised.
- The fail-toward-silence posture for conjunction births may be retired, refined, or
  formalized as the correct ceremonial response depending on the tradition's answer.

## Provenance

Surfaced in the v3 unknown-unknowns audit, June 2026. Venus phase computation existed
in the architecture prior to this audit, but conjunction handling was never addressed.
The current code's behavior on conjunction birth dates is unreviewed and provisional.
