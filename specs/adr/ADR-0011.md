# ADR-0011: OPEN — Chol Q'ij Day-Boundary Semantics

- **Status:** proposed — OPEN, pending Ajq'ij review
- **Date:** 2026-06-10
- **Authority:** Flagged for Ajq'ij review — this is a lineage question, not an
  engineering decision
- **Supersedes:** —

## Context

The Chol Q'ij is a continuously running 260-day cycle. To assign a nahual and
trecena number to a birth time, the calendar engine must know when one day ends and
the next begins. At least three conventions exist:

1. **Midnight local time** — the seeker's local timezone
2. **Midnight Guatemala time** — the geographic origin of the K'iche' tradition
3. **Sunrise** — the convention used in many Mesoamerican ceremonial traditions,
   where the day is understood to begin at dawn rather than at midnight

These conventions produce different nahuales for seekers born near any midnight
boundary. A seeker born at 11:40 p.m. receives one nahual under midnight conventions
and a different nahual under a sunrise convention — a different Cruz Maya, a different
life-reading, a different cosmological portrait.

This is not a preference question or a UX decision. It is a question about how the
tradition understands the boundary of a sacred day. The code must answer it, because
the code cannot avoid answering it — silence in the code means a silent default, and
a silent default is exactly what this project's architecture is designed to prevent.

## Why this is flagged, not decided

The v3 unknown-unknowns audit (Territory 3, "The Gremlins") identified this as the
deepest class of unknown unknown in the project: a lineage question hiding inside an
engineering ticket. The ADR process requires it to be asked explicitly and answered
by lineage authority before the code answers it by default.

## Current behavior (until this ADR is accepted)

Per the fail-toward-silence principle (ADR-0009), the calendar engine must not
silently assign a nahual for birth times where the convention choice would change the
result. Specifically:

**For any birth time within two hours of any midnight boundary, the engine must
fail-toward-silence rather than return a potentially incorrect nahual.** The in-register
response is: *"The boundary of this day in your birth time is something the old words
settle differently in different places. I cannot assign a nahual here without knowing
which way you were told. If you know your tradition's practice, tell me; otherwise
we will work with what is clear."*

This is a temporary posture until Ajq'ij review resolves the question.

## Questions for Ajq'ij review

1. Does the K'iche' ceremonial day begin at sunrise, at midnight local time, or at
   midnight Guatemala time?
2. Is there a single authoritative answer, or does this vary by lineage line and
   geographic community?
3. For a seeker outside Guatemala (e.g., in New York, in Tokyo), which timezone
   governs — their local time or Guatemala time?
4. What is the correct ceremonial response when a seeker's birth time sits on a
   boundary — is there a tradition for this ambiguity?

## Consequences once accepted

- A superseding ADR will record the Ajq'ij's answer, their name and authority, and
  the date of the consultation.
- The calendar engine will be updated to implement the accepted convention.
- The golden test suite will add boundary-case assertions.
- The fail-toward-silence posture for near-boundary births may be retired or refined
  based on the traditional handling of boundary ambiguity.
