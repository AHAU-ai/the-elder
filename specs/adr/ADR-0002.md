# ADR-0002: Always "the Pythia of Delphi," Never "the Oracle of Delphi"

- **Status:** accepted
- **Date:** 2026-06-10
- **Authority:** Founders
- **Supersedes:** —

## Context

Two names exist for the Greek prophetic voice: "the Oracle of Delphi" and "the Pythia
of Delphi." The Oracle refers to the institution — the site, the god, the pronouncement
itself. The Pythia refers to the human vessel: the woman who held the role, breathed
the vapors, and spoke. THE ELDER's design principle is that voices are carriers of
tradition-shaped language, not institutions or deities. The human vessel is the correct
address because it is the honest one: a person speaking from within a field, not an
authoritative institution pronouncing from above it.

"The Oracle of Delphi" also risks implying that the instrument itself is oracular —
an overclaim the claim hierarchy (strategy v2) explicitly forecloses.

## Decision

The Greek voice is always named "the Pythia of Delphi." The phrase "Oracle of Delphi"
is never used to name this voice in any prompt, UI copy, white paper, or public
communication. The word "oracle" in general use (e.g., "oracular register") is
acceptable when clearly descriptive, not nominal.

## Consequences

- Every voice spec, system prompt, and UI string must use "the Pythia of Delphi."
- The voice regression harness (voiceRegression.ts) already includes `/oracle of delphi/i`
  as a forbidden-leakage pattern; this ADR is the authority behind that regex.
- Copy review before any public release must check for this phrase.

## Provenance

Established during voice-naming sessions; treated as a standing rule throughout all
subsequent architecture work. Confirmed in strategy v1–v3 and the voice regression
harness implementation.
